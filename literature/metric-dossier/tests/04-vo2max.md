# VO2max (maximal aerobic capacity)

## What it measures

The most oxygen the body can use per minute during all-out exercise, usually in mL per kg body weight per minute. Measured by breathing through a mask on a treadmill or bike while effort rises to exhaustion (cardiopulmonary exercise test, CPET). The best single number for heart–lung–muscle fitness.

## Reference population

- Age norms: 3,816 healthy Norwegian men and women aged 20–90 (HUNT fitness study, Loe 2013), treadmill, direct gas analysis. Their equipment's test-retest correlation was 0.99, CV 1.8% (lab check on staff, not on participants).
- Day-to-day noise: 742 healthy adults tested on two days (HERITAGE, as analysed by Shephard 2004, citing Katch's earlier work `[to verify]` attribution).

## Noise

| Study | Population | ICC | Day-to-day CV / error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shephard 2004 (HERITAGE re-analysis) | 742 healthy adults, 2 days | — | CV 5.0% before training, 4.1% after |
| Edgett 2018 | Young recreationally active adults, 3 tests each | 0.938–0.992 | CV 4.9% |
| PMC10532016 `[to verify]` citation | Active women | 0.984 | CV 1.98%, SEM 0.77 mL/kg/min |
| Review (Beltz 2016 `[to verify]` DOI) | Meta-analysis by Vickers | — | Mean standard error 2.58 mL/kg/min; total ±5.6%, of which ~93% biological, ~7% machine |

- In plain English: one person's VO2max moves about **3–5% between days** with no real change. For an adult at ~40 mL/kg/min, a change under roughly 2–4 mL/kg/min is noise. The smallest real change for one person must be computed from these (≈ 2.77 × SEM) once the primary SEM is read. `[to verify]`
- Most of that noise is **the body, not the machine**: day-to-day biology (sleep, food, hydration, motivation) dominates.
- Shephard's point: with a 5% day-to-day CV, much of the apparent "non-response" to training in group studies is just test-retest error. Same applies to the president's readings.

## Known failure modes

- **Not reaching true maximum** (effort-limited stop). Rule: a verification bout after the ramp test; agreement within ~2–3% confirms a true maximum (meta-analysis, PLOS ONE 2021 `[to verify]` DOI).
- **Estimated VO2max is not VO2max.** Sub-maximal or formula-based estimates (step tests, cycle formulas, wearables) err by roughly 8–22% against measured values (several studies `[to verify]`). Only a direct gas-analysis CPET counts as this input.
- Treadmill gives higher values than bike; mode must be fixed.
- Heat raises day-to-day error (CV 9.6% in one study `[to verify]`).

## Functional anchor and age trajectory

## Confounders

Training status (changes in weeks), body weight (per-kg value moves with weight), sex, altitude, heat, sleep, recent illness, beta-blockers. `[to verify]` list.
