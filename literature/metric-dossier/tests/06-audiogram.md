# Audiogram (pure-tone hearing thresholds)

## What it measures

The quietest tone a person can hear at each pitch (frequency), per ear, in decibels hearing level (dB HL). Standard range 0.25–8 kHz; extended high frequencies (EHF) 9–16 kHz are where age-related loss starts first.

## Reference population for the noise figures

- Normal-hearing adults, standard frequencies and EHF up to 16 kHz, two earphone types (Schmuziger 2004). n and ages `[to verify]`.
- Adults tested twice with hearing-aid in-situ audiometry (Smith-Olinde 2006).
- Remote home audiometry under varying ambient noise (Thoidis 2024) — tablet, not clinic booth.
- Neurotypical controls in a Down syndrome retest study (Porter 2025), sessions repeated over months.

## Noise

| Study | Setting | Test-retest result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clinical standard (StatPearls; BSA and ASHA procedures) | Booth, adults | ±5 dB expected; retest at 1 kHz differing by >5 dB triggers a recheck |
| Schmuziger 2004 | Booth, same session | Within 10 dB for >99% of measurements at 0.5–6 kHz, >94% at 8–16 kHz |
| Smith-Olinde 2006 | In situ, adults | Mean difference <1 dB; within 5 dB for 93–100% depending on frequency |
| Thoidis 2024 | Remote tablet | Mean absolute difference 4.7 dB (3.7–6.2 by frequency); 74% within 5 dB, 90% within 10 dB |
| Porter 2025 | Repeated over months | Same-session ≤10 dB for 94–100%; across sessions up to 11 months, 15–36% of differences >10 dB (part of it real change) |

- In plain English: one threshold moving by **10 dB or less** between two tests is within noise. A shift of **≥10 dB at one frequency, or ≥5 dB averaged over several adjacent frequencies, repeated on a second test**, is the usual signal of real change `[to verify]` exact criterion (ototoxicity-monitoring rules, e.g. ASHA).
- EHF thresholds (where early ageing shows) are noisier than standard ones; use the same earphone model every time.
- Test steps are 5 dB, so the instrument itself cannot resolve smaller changes.

## Known failure modes

- Background noise outside a booth raises thresholds (Thoidis 2024).
- Earphone placement and type change EHF thresholds.
- Earwax, colds and recent loud-noise exposure cause temporary shifts.
- Learning and attention: first tests can read slightly worse.

## Functional anchor and age trajectory

Hearing is itself the function. Age-related loss starts at the highest frequencies and spreads down. `[to verify]` population norms (e.g. ISO 7029) before quoting numbers.

## Confounders

Noise exposure (work, music, the 48 h before testing), ear infections, earwax, ototoxic drugs, sex, earphone type, room noise.

## Blind spot

The audiogram does not measure hearing in noise or "hidden" synaptic loss. Listed as partial cover of the hearing axis.
