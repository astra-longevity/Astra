# Grip strength (handheld dynamometer)

## What it measures

Maximum squeezing force of one hand, in kilograms, usually the best of 2–3 tries per hand. A cheap stand-in for whole-body muscle strength.

## Reference population for the noise figures

- CLSA (Canadian Longitudinal Study on Aging) retest subsample, n = 146, community-dwelling Canadians (Beauchamp 2021). Age range of the subsample `[to verify]`.
- Healthy adults, any age: pooled meta-analysis of test-retest studies (Journal of Sports Science and Medicine, `[to verify]` DOI).

## Noise

| Study | Population | ICC | SEM | Smallest real change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beauchamp 2021 (CLSA) | n = 146, community-dwelling | 0.95 (0.93–0.97) | 2.71 kg | 6.3 kg (90% confidence) |
| Upper-limb injury study, healthy group `[to verify]` source | n = 109 healthy | 0.94 (single try); 0.95 (mean of 2) | 2.7 kg; 2.3 kg | 7.4 kg (95%); 6.4 kg with mean of 2 tries |

- In plain English: a person's grip must move by about **6–7 kg** between two sessions before we can call it a real change. Averaging tries shrinks this a little.
- A meta-analysis on healthy populations found neither age, test-retest interval nor device type significantly changed absolute reliability (`[to verify]` DOI).
- Device matters when switching: Jamar and Jamar Plus+ agree closely (ICC 0.96–0.98 in young adults); a Biodex dynamometer does not agree with handheld devices (limits of agreement up to ±73%) (dos Santos 2025). Rule: **one device model for all of one person's readings.**

## Known failure modes

- Hand pain, arthritis or a recent hand injury lowers the reading without ageing.
- Effort and instructions: an unmotivated try reads low; protocol (posture, elbow angle, number of tries) must be fixed.

## Functional anchor and age trajectory

Grip is itself a functional test. Age trajectory: peak in early adulthood, then decline. `[to verify]` population norms (e.g. Dodds 2014 UK norms) before quoting numbers.

## Confounders

Sex, body size, hand dominance, hand injury, device model, time of day, training.
