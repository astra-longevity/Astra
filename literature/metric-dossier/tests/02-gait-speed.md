# Gait speed (usual walking pace)

## What it measures

How fast a person walks over a short straight course (often 4 m) at their normal pace, in metres per second.

## Reference population for the noise figures

- CLSA retest subsample, n = 147, community-dwelling Canadians, mean speed 0.90 m/s (Beauchamp 2021). Age range `[to verify]`.
- Irish adults aged 50 and over, retest after 1–4 months (Donoghue 2019, BMJ Open). n `[to verify]`.

## Noise

| Study | Population | ICC | SEM | Smallest real change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beauchamp 2021 (CLSA) | n = 147 | 0.64 (0.54–0.73) | 0.10 m/s | 0.23 m/s (90%) |
| Donoghue 2019 | Irish, 50+, 1–4 months apart | 0.88 (usual pace) | `[to verify]` | 0.19–0.35 m/s across walking tests |

- In plain English: on a 4-m course, a change smaller than about **0.2 m/s** (roughly a fifth of a normal pace) can be pure noise, given a short course timed with a stopwatch.
- Donoghue found most of the variation came from **who ran the test** (rater), not time of day. Rule: same rater, same course, same instructions.
- Longer or instrumented walkways report much lower noise (one study: ICC 0.98–0.99, smallest real change 0.09–0.13 m/s) `[to verify]` source and population.

## Known failure modes

- Short courses: timing a 4-m walk by stopwatch adds error.
- Acceleration phase included or not changes the speed; protocol must be fixed.
- Ceiling effect in fit middle-aged adults: little room to detect decline. `[to verify]`

## Functional anchor and age trajectory

Gait speed is itself functional and predicts survival in older adults (Studenski 2011 `[to verify]` DOI). Age trajectory: roughly flat to the 60s, then declines. `[to verify]` norms.

## Confounders

Height and leg length, footwear, pain, rater, course length, mood and motivation.
